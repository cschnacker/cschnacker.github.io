How to use Easy W-4 · instructions & example

Start with what you earn. Then choose what to withhold.

  1. Enter your household. Choose your filing status and state. Enter actual dependents, interest, dividends, and other income once. Use “Other taxable income” only for amounts not entered in another category. A spouse and the filer are not “other dependents.”
  2. Add each job. Select Job 1–4 and enter salary, bonus, pay frequency, work state, and benefits. Choose one entry method for each retirement plan: percent, per-paycheck dollars, or annual dollars. Type an exact amount or use the field arrows.
  3. Choose federal W-4 entries. The household amounts are references. “Still available to enter” subtracts entries on all four W-4s. Step 3 shows child and other-dependent counts; the summary converts them to annual credit dollars. The actual W-4 uses dollar amounts. Follow the IRS instructions before submitting a form.
  4. Review state withholding. The wages-and-bonuses option approximates withholding from the imported state tax tables. Other household income still counts toward annual state tax and can leave a balance due. Choose Manual to enter actual payroll withholding. California also offers a DE 4 estimate. Different residence and work states require manual withholding in this version.
  5. Read the results. Paycheck estimates are for the selected job. “At tax time” combines all jobs and household income. It assumes a full year at the entered pay, not a midyear catch-up calculation. Extra withholding reduces the estimated balance due.
  6. Save and return. Entries automatically save by tax year in this browser profile. Export inputs for backup or transfer. Reset clears the saved inputs for the active year. Tax data is supplied with each app version.

WORKED EXAMPLE · TWO JOBS

One household amount, shared across W-4s

Suppose your household has $12,000 of taxable interest. Enter $12,000 once under Adjustments to income. You can explore splitting Step 4(a) between two jobs:

ActionRemaining on every W-4
Before W-4 entries$12,000
Enter $7,000 on Job 1$5,000
Enter $2,000 on Job 2$3,000
Add the last $3,000 to either job$0

The same shared balance applies to dependents and additional deductions. For example, 3 eligible children less 1 entered on Job 1 and 1 on Job 2 leaves 1. A $10,000 deduction reference less $6,000 and $1,000 leaves $3,000.

This illustrates the calculator’s allocation, not a recommendation to split your forms this way. IRS multiple-job instructions generally place Steps 3–4(b) on one W-4, preferably for the highest-paying job. Check the official instructions for your situation.

Entering interest on a federal W-4 does not create new household income or automatically increase state withholding. The actual $12,000 remains in annual tax estimates even if Step 4(a) is left blank.

Use real dependent counts in the household section. Larger planning entries on a W-4 do not make someone an eligible dependent. Mortgage-interest deductions belong in itemized deductions and, where applicable, Step 4(b), rather than invented dependents.
