Calculation changes

Source: Easy_W-4_0.45-2026.xlsx. Original files are unchanged.

Repairs in this version

  • Income and live W-4 balances: Other taxable household income is included once in annual federal and state estimates. W-4 entries remain independent. Prominent remaining balances subtract entries on all four W-4s as you type. Every job shows the same shared remaining balance for children, other dependents, income, and deductions. Annual results show state tax and withholding separately; Estimated withholding covers wages and bonuses; uncovered tax on other income remains in the year-end balance.
  • Suggested state withholding: New scenarios default to an estimate of state tax on wages and bonuses using the imported annual state tax model and household filing state, with non-payroll income removed for withholding only. Allocate that wage-and-bonus liability in proportion to regular wages after modeled pretax deductions; divide each share by that job’s pay periods. Bonus tax is included in regular-paycheck estimates; tax on interest, dividends, and other non-payroll income remains in annual liability and may produce a balance due, with no separate bonus withholding in Suggested mode. Extra withholding is additive. Manual and California DE 4 modes remain available; mixing modes can create a balance due or refund. Multistate employment requires manual entries; households without positive taxable regular wages retain an unallocated balance. This does not certify the underlying state tax data or predict payroll withholding.
  • Independent state withholding: Every job now has separate federal and state choices. California uses EDD 2026 Method B annualized tables and its own DE 4 status, regular allowances, deduction allowances, extra withholding, and exemption. Bonus estimates assume the separately paid bonus flat-rate method. Other states use explicit manual withholding entries pending verified state-specific implementations; they no longer inherit federal W-4 entries. Imported older scenarios start with single/zero California allowances or zero manual withholding elsewhere and require state-entry review. Annual state tax liability still uses the imported workbook model. Taxable wage treatment remains simplified, including the combined HSA/flexible spending input; California SDI is not modeled.
  • Retirement contributions: Each job uses one method per plan: whole-number percent, dollars per paycheck, or annual dollars. Inactive entries are retained but excluded from calculations. Older scenarios infer the first nonzero method in that order; review the selection after importing.
  • W-4 guidance: Household-based reference amounts for children, other dependents, other income and additional deductions appear beside independent W-4 choices. References subtract the combined entries on all four jobs, so claims can be split among W-4s in any order. Choices may exceed the references without changing household facts or triggering a dependent-count warning.
  • Year selection: A3 follows the loaded annual dataset instead of TODAY(), preventing future dates from silently breaking lookups.
  • Jobs 2–4 retirement deductions: C114, C145 and C176 divided by blank cells D81, E81 and F81. Each now uses its own pay frequency.
  • Jobs 2–4 withholding income: C117, C148 and C179 now deduct the job's health/HSA amounts once and retirement contributions once.
  • Jobs 3–4 benefits and dependent credits: B249/B250 now use the correct job; C149/C180 and G247:G250 no longer use the next job's credit/filing cells.
  • All pay frequencies and state filing statuses: E37/F37 and E51/F51 now recalculate rather than retaining fixed values.
  • W-4 Step 3: child credit amounts use FEqn!I4 instead of the education credit in P4. Other dependent credits now reduce withholding for all jobs. Extra withholding is added after credits are applied.
  • Take-home pay: C98/C129/C160/C191 no longer subtract state credits a second time. Jobs 2–4 now subtract their actual after-tax deductions instead of empty row 626.
  • Federal brackets: the first-bracket calculation in C222:C226 incorrectly returned zero below the first threshold. A shared marginal-bracket calculation applies the correct rate across every band.
  • State brackets: repeated source expressions used the preceding band’s rate when crossing several thresholds. A shared marginal-bracket calculation now matches each band to its own rate.
  • State withholding: each job uses its own state, filing category, deductions, credits, and pay frequency.
  • Deductions: L270:L274 no longer add tax credits to itemized deductions. B254 respects the itemizing choice and handles zero wages.
  • Pension income: O254:O258 no longer include the same pension twice through both D and K income columns.
  • Bonuses: annual withholding totals now include bonus withholding from all four jobs. State bonus estimates use marginal brackets instead of legacy curve coefficients, which included malformed text for Massachusetts.
  • Other credits: B262 sums credits from all four jobs rather than only job 1.
  • Utah credit data: source strings “$966 credit” and “$1932 credit” were normalized into numeric credit fields with a credit marker, using the workbook’s amounts. Those amounts still require annual policy review.
  • Social Security: annual-average payroll tax respects the wage base; bonus tax uses the remaining wage base. The 2026 annual data field was corrected from $168,600 to $184,500, as published by SSA.
  • Error visibility: paycheck outputs no longer replace calculation errors with zero.

Annual data and remaining tax review

Federal tax, federal withholding, all 51 state/DC tables, and policy constants are separate from the interface and formulas. The Annual data screen edits these values and exports a year-specific JSON file.

This is a modernization of the supplied model, not certification of every tax rule. Source rules for earned income, education, dependent care, Social Security benefits, retirement deductions, capital gains, and itemization remain simplified. The source’s child age categories, SALT/medical rules, and most state values need review. Additional Medicare tax, self-employment tax, midyear withholding, and the complete 2026 W-4 deduction worksheet are not modeled. Multiple employers/persons and multistate returns require further handling. A full-year estimate may differ from an actual return.

Use IRS Publication 15-T, the current W-4 instructions, and each state’s guidance when reviewing an annual release. Existing withholding tables were imported from the workbook and have not all been reconciled to official publications.

Verification

The unmodified calculation model reproduces all 697 saved formula values/errors in the supplied workbook. Regression tests separately cover the documented repairs, marginal tax thresholds, four-job consistency, annual-data changes, and year rollover. Saved zero-input values are a compatibility baseline; they do not establish tax-law accuracy.
