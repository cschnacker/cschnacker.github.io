INDEPENDENT PLANNING TOOL

Understand your estimate.

This calculator is not affiliated with or approved by the U.S. government. It does not determine eligibility, submit applications, or provide an official benefit quote.

Separate methods for each person

Choose a method for the primary person and a different method for the spouse. Spousal benefit only assumes the spouse has no own worker benefit; Exclude spouse benefits removes the spouse from the projection. Here, “SS-taxed earnings” means the Taxed Social Security Earnings on an earnings record, not Medicare earnings. Work that did not pay Social Security tax is excluded.

Three ways to plan

Quick estimate: Enter the age you turn this year, one year's Social Security-taxed earnings, and an annual growth assumption. The planner estimates past covered earnings, limits each year to its Social Security wage cap, indexes earnings, and selects the highest 35 indexed years. A smooth growth rate will not reproduce a real career.

Closer estimate: Enter the Taxed Social Security Earnings column from an earnings record, including older years if available. Those recorded years replace guessed wages. Years before the last recorded year that are not supplied count as zero; later years may be projected if the person keeps working. The planner indexes earnings before age 60 and selects the highest 35 indexed years. A wage report alone does not guarantee the exact benefit SSA will pay.

SSA-reported estimate: Enter the monthly amounts shown by SSA in today's dollars for age 67 and age 70. The planner uses the selected amount directly and separately projects future dollars using the COLA assumption. SSA also offers future-dollar estimates, which should not be entered in this mode. Actual COLAs and benefits may differ.

Limits of the estimates

The one-year and earnings-history paths are independent projections. They use published wage caps, average wage indexes, historical COLAs, and a projected benefit formula for the birth-year cohort derived from plan year and age. Claiming before full retirement age reduces the worker benefit; delaying up to age 70 increases it. The spouse's own worker benefit is combined with a possible spousal supplement. The supplement is based on the excess of half the primary worker's unreduced benefit over the spouse's unreduced worker benefit; it does not use the primary worker's delayed credits. It begins only after the primary worker claims and is permanently reduced if the spouse first qualifies before full retirement age. Future earnings growth, wage indexes, and COLAs are assumptions. Results can differ from SSA because of missing or corrected earnings, special eligibility rules, exact claiming month, rounding, and later benefit adjustments. The annual amounts in the one-year and history paths are full-year equivalents in the first claiming year. The planner does not verify work credits, child-in-care exceptions, family maximums, or survivor benefits.

Annual data and privacy

Historical wage caps, wage indexes, and benefit COLAs are kept in one editable annual data file. Calculation happens locally in your browser. Inputs, including an earnings history, stay in this browser on this device until you use Clear saved values or clear site data. CSV exports and printed plans contain the numbers you entered.
